Making a subliminal

This guide goes from blank page to posted video: writing your script, recording it, mixing it and exporting it.

1. Write your affirmations

Most creators follow a few simple rules:

  • Present tense. “I am confident”, not “I will be confident”.
  • Positive wording. Say what you want, not what you don't: “I sleep deeply”, not “I don't wake up at night”.
  • Short and specific. One idea per sentence makes the recording easier to layer and the on-screen text easier to read.
  • Twenty to fifty lines is a comfortable script. The studio repeats it to fill the length you choose.

2. Record your voice

Your phone's voice memo app is enough. A few habits make a big difference:

  • Record in a small, soft room. A closet full of clothes is a classic home vocal booth.
  • Hold the phone about a hand's width from your mouth, slightly to the side so your breath doesn't hit it.
  • Speak at an even, calm pace. Leave a short breath between lines.
  • Trim long silences at the start and end. The studio adds its own pause between repeats.

The studio accepts MP3, M4A, WAV, AAC, OGG, Opus, FLAC and WebM, or a video file with sound.

3. Make it calmer

Voice tone works on your recording as soon as you add it:

  • Natural keeps it as recorded. The studio only trims silence and evens out the peak level.
  • Calm quiets background hiss and hum between phrases, softens harsh and hissy sounds, evens out loud and quiet words, and adds a soft room so it feels relaxed rather than close-miked.
  • Dreamy goes further, with a warmer sound and a longer, airier echo.

The echo adds well under a second after your last word so it can fade out naturally.

4. Choose the voice level

The voice level sets how far your recording sits under the background, in decibels. Every 6 dB lower is roughly half as loud.

Judge it on headphones at the volume your listeners will use. Phone speakers hide quiet detail.

5. Layers, speed and length

Layers add extra copies of your recording that start at different points and spread across left and right. Two or three layers give a fuller, harder-to-pick-apart murmur. Five is dense.

Speed plays the recording faster, from 1× to 3×, so more affirmations fit in each minute. Higher speeds also raise the pitch.

Length is Recording length by default: the video plays your recording once and ends, so it's never longer than what you recorded. The background fades in and out, but your voice is never faded or cut. If you want a longer track, choose Repeat the voice for a number of repeats, or Set minutes to loop the voice until the time is filled.

6. Pick the background sound

You can layer several sounds. Some pairings that work well:

  • Rain + dream pad for sleep and calm.
  • Ocean + wind for an open, airy feel.
  • Brown noise alone for focus. Its low rumble masks the voice well.
  • Fireplace + pad for something cosy.

Theta 6 Hz is a binaural beat: a slightly different tone in each ear. It only works on headphones. On speakers it sounds like a plain hum.

If you add your own music, make sure you have the rights to it. TikTok, Instagram and YouTube often mute or block videos that contain copyrighted tracks. The built-in sounds are generated in your browser and are safe to use.

7. Visuals and text

There are fifteen moving scenes: sparkles, aurora, night sky, voice orb, waveform, rain, snow, petals, bubbles, fireflies, hearts, bokeh lights, a moonlit sea, clouds, or your own photo. Each one works with all seven colour palettes.

Your words on screen

With From my recording selected, the studio writes out what you say and shows each line while you're saying it. Pick the language you speak in, then check the lines: speech recognition can mishear words, and you can fix any line by typing over it. If you speed the voice up, the words keep pace.

The first time, your browser downloads a speech model of about 100 MB. After that it's kept on your device. The recording itself never leaves your device. If you'd rather show something else, choose My own lines and type affirmations that cycle on screen.

Tips

  • Voice orb and Waveform move with your recording, so viewers can see that something is playing even when they can't hear it.
  • If you use your photo, darken it a little so the title stays readable.
  • Keep the title short (two to four words) and put the format (“subliminal · headphones on”) in the small line under it.
  • Your own lines cycle in the middle of the frame. Five to eight seconds each is comfortable to read.

8. Export

Press Create video and keep the page open until it finishes. Rendering happens on your device:

  • Chrome, Edge and Safari encode H.264 video with AAC sound. That's the most compatible MP4 and it renders faster than real time.
  • Some browsers can only pack the sound as Opus. YouTube accepts that. For Instagram, render in Chrome on Android, Edge or Safari instead.
  • Browsers without the fast encoder record in real time, so a 10-minute video takes 10 minutes.
  • On a phone, choose 720p for long videos and keep the screen on.

Audio only exports just the mix, which is handy for playlists or podcast apps.
